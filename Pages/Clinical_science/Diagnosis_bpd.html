Introduction

This condition is characterised by a pervasive, long-term pattern across several contexts of significant interpersonal relationship instability, extreme fear of abandonment, and intense emotional outbursts. It often results in self-harm or engagement in risky behaviours, primarily caused by trouble regulating emotions. Even dissociation, a sense of emptiness, and a distorted sense of self are prevalent. Comorbidity exists with substance use disorders, depressive disorders, and eating disorders. Roughly 10% of patients die by suicide, while roughly 50-80% engage in self-harm (of which 70% without any intention to end their lives).

A core feature is emotion dysregulation, which may involve hypersensitivity to emotional stimuli, heightened emotional intensity, large and rapid mood shifts, tendency for negative emotions, high affective empathy but low cognitive empathy, and a slow return to baseline after emotional arousal. It often leads to the development of maladaptive ccoping strategies. Related, is the feature of dissociation, which may occur to disconnect from physical or emotional experiences, and lead to a sense of emotional detachment. Difficulty with interpersonal relationships is another hallmark feature, often caused by the idealisation and devaludation of others. This pattern is referred to as splitting, which may occur as external splitting (i.e., doing this to others) and internal splitting (i.e., doing this to oneself). While indeed there is a strong desire for intimacy, insecure, avoidant, ambivalent and fearfully pre-occupied attachment styles prevent this. Lastly, manipulative behaviour is rather common, with 88% of therapists having experienced it from BPD patients.

Because of their unstable self-concept, BPD patients often adapt a strategy called "mirroring" in which they take on their sense of self from their surroundings. This may often lead to emotional contagion!

The idea of psychosis amongst BPD patients is complex, but phenomenlogical study has shown that at least their hallucinatory experiences are indistinguishable from those in actual psychosis, hence providing the insight that it should be treated as a real experience.

Aetiology & pathophysiology

Studies point to a heritability of 37-69%, with roughly 42% of feature variability explained by genetic factors. Within personality disorders, BPD is the third most heritable, with the consensus being that these are generally more heritable than Axis I (e.g., depression and eating disorders) conditions. However, there is also a strong correlation between BPD and adverse childhood experiences (notably (sexual) abuse and loss of caregiver). Linehan's theory is that "BPD arises from the interaction between a child's inherent emotional vulnerability and an invalidating environment—an environment characterized by the neglect, ridicule, dismissal, or discouragement of a child's emotions and needs.".

Indeed, neuroimaging studies show reduced volume in the hippocampus, orbitofrontal cortex, anterior cingulate cortex, and amygdala. Indeed, there seems to be a correlation between executive function and severity of impact of social rejection on BPD symptoms. This might well be why ADHD and ASD are seen as risk factors.

Diagnosis and treatment

Millon proposed four subtypes:

  1. Discouraged: Including avoidant, depressive, and dependent features || Pliant, submissive, loyal, humble; feels vulnerable and in constant jeopardy; feels hopeless, depressed, helpless, and powerless.
  2. Impulsive: Including histrionic and antisocial features || Capricious, superficial, flighty, distractible, frenetic, and seductive; fearing loss, becomes agitated, and gloomy and irritable; potentially suicidal.
  3. Petulant: Including negativistic features || Negativistic, impactient, restless, as well as stubborn, defiant, sullen, pessimistic, and resentful; easily slighted and quickly disillusioned.
  4. Self-destructive: Including depressive and masochistic features || Inward-turning, intropunitively angry; conforming, deferential, and integratiating behaviours have deteriorated; increasingly high-strung and moody; possibly suicidal.

75% of BPD patients experience concurrent mood disorders, and roughly the same amount also fulfill the criteria for a concurrent personality disorder.

Women are diagnosed up to 3 times as often. Sexual minorities are up to 4 times more likely to be diagnosed with BPD, and gender minorities even higher. Partially to blame here is the differential presentation (men with BPD are more likely to present with substance use, explosive temper, novelty seeking, antisocial-narcissistic / passive-aggressive / sadistic personality traits i.e., antisocial overtones), while women are more likely to present iwht eating, mood, anxiety and post-traumatic stress disorders prompting them to more quickly reach out for help.

Crisis

Een crisis gaat gepaard met grote emotionele onrust, en een daaruit voortvloeiende onzekerheid. Hier kan dialectische gedragstherapie in ondersteunen. Ter voorkoming van deze crises wordt vaak een signaleringsplan/crisiskaart ingezet, waarin het onstaan van een crisis wordt besproken en gekozen wordt voor opname-voorkomende interventies (IHT/IBT) of kortdurende opname op een HIC. In dit laatste geval, bed-op-recept, moet er een duidelijk behandelplan aan te grondslag liggen waarbij de autonomie van patiënt wordt gerespecteerd en er gelegenheid is tot dagelijkse gesprekken. Hierbij wordt uitgegaan van de eigen mogelijkheden en kwaliteiten van de patiënt. Om dit te bewerkstelligen gaan HIC-verpleegkundigen een intensieve hulpverlenersrelatie met de patiënt aan in zijn of haar dagelijks leven. Contacten met andere instanties, zoals politie en ambulancedienst, maar zeker ook met naasten behoren tot de taken van HIC-verpleegkundigen. Deze contacten hebben niet alleen een grote meerwaarde in het dagelijkse werk, maar dragen ook bij aan een sneller herstel van de patiënt. Voor een sneller herstel zijn in het bijzonder contacten met naasten van belang.

The NICE guidelines (2009) state that the physician should use empathetic, open questioning, including validating statements; seek to stimulate reflection about solutions; avoid minimising; and refrain from offering solutions before receiving full clarification of the problems. When prescribing sedatives, choose one that has minimal risk of addiction or misuse, e.g., a sedative antihistamine, and ensure that it is stopped as soon as possible. This might also be useful in the management of insomnia, prevalent in BPD crises.
