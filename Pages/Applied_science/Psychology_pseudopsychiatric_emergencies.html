Introduction

Up to 10% of psychiatric emergencies are actually causes by underlying organic causes. In general, the three main groups that mimic psychiatric disorders are 1) endocrine and metabolic disorders, 2) internal diseases, and 3) neurologic disorders.

Endocrine and Metabolic Disorders

In general, remember this rule:

  1. Central:
    1. Hypo-thalamic disorders: bulimia or anorexia, hypersomnia, impotency, and anxiety attacks
    2. Hypo-pituitary disorders: dementia and delirium
    3. Hyper-prolactinemia: mood, behaviour, and eating disorders (but treatment with dopamine agonists may cause psychosis; which may be treated with dopamine antagonists which cause hyperprolactinemia)
    4. Pituitary adenoma: mass effect may cause hallucinations (visual or olfactory), episodes of "losing time", and apathy
  2. Peripheral:
    1. Hypothyroidism: high impairment of recent memory, lack of initiative, diminished learning ability, and speech deficits. Later, it may lead to letergy and even coma.
    2. Hyperthyroidism: exophthalmos (!) but critically emotive lability, exasperated anxiety, possibly developing into bipolar, paranoia, or psychosis; persistance after rebalancing may be immune-mediated.
    3. Pheochromocytoma (hyper-adrenalinemia): panick attack
    4. Addison's disease (hypo-cortisolemia): chronic anorexia, depression, and psychosis (hyperpigmentation, reduced sodium-potassium ratio, lymphocytosis, eosinophilia!)
    5. Cushing's disease (hyper-cortisolemia): 50% have depressive symptoms (weight gain, buffalo hump, purple striae)

Even dehydration could cause confusion in the elderly, and heatstroke may also lead to neuropsychiatric dysfunction with bizarre behaviour. Then, there are the metabolic disorders:

  1. Hypo-natremia: seizures and confusion up to coma
  2. Hyper-natremia: presents with abnormal cognitive status and impaired neuromuscular function
  3. Hypo-calcemia: tetany (eendenbekhand; main d'accoucheur) and panick attacks
  4. Hyper-calcemia: depression, confusion, and psychosis

There are various vitamin deficiencies, but important to know are thiamine (Wernicke-Korsakoff) and nicotinamide (pellagra encephalopathy). Zinc, then, when deficient may lead to depression.

Internal diseases

It names first hypoxemia and hypercarbia. Together with various pulmonary diseases, they lead to panick attacks and mood dysregulation especially depression. Even anemia may provide a pseudopsychiatric picture!

Celiac disease may provide a risk factor for depressive disorders and schizophrenia, as is IBD for sleep disorders, anxiety, and depression. Crohn's similarly is a risk factor. Remember liver encephalopathy, leading to apathy and eventually coma. Acute renal failure may present as sleep disorders, eventually leading to agitation or confusion. Altered mental status in the elderly may result from retention! The hypothesis is the "cystocerebral syndrome" where adrenergic tension builds up when micturition may not occur.

Psoriasis too, may be related through inflammation.

When looking at infection, "disease behaviour" (pro-inflammatory cytokines meant to combat disease) may be implicated in the precipitation of psychiatric symptoms. Here, we see e.g., SLE, Sjögren's, Behçet's, and even RA causing depression, inhibition, anorexia etc. The PANDAS group are children post-streptococcal infection developing OCD. Various infections may reach the brain: brucellosis, malaria, borreliosis.

Neurologic disorders

Here we see neoplasia, metal poisoning, and head trauma.
