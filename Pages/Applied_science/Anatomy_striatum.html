The striatum describes a collection of structures united not so much in function, but mostly in the fact that all appear striated / streaked when observed anatomically. Together, they make up the largest part of the basal ganglia which consist of:

  1. Striatum
  2. Globus pallidus / ventral pallidum
  3. Substantia nigra
  4. Subthalamic nucleus

Together, they may be imaged as "wrapping around" the thalami: This thus also means we have two of each: two thalami, two sets of basal ganglia etc. Notably, the substantia nigra is also part of the midbrain / mesencephalon tegmentum. The striatum proper consists of two main structures:

  1. Dorsal striatum
  2. Ventral striatum

Which each consist of two further structures: (Doric Columns Put Vast Accents On Top)

  1. Dorsal striatum ("Doric")
    1. Nucleus Caudatus ("Columns")
    2. Putamen ("Put")
  2. Ventral striatum ("Vast")
    1. Nucleus accumbens ("Accents")
    2. Olfactory tubercle ("On Top")

NB: the names "dorsal" and "ventral" only make sense when you throw your head back into your neck, like an animal does when walking on four legs. Otherwise they're more superior-inferior related As a generalisation, we may say the following: "the ventral striatum is connected to the ventral tegmental area (VTA) and thus Very TeachAble i.e., involved in reward, learning and addiction, while the dorsal striatum is dopamine-oriented and thus connected to both dopamine centres (VTA and substantia nigra) and hence involved in both learning, reward and addiction, as well as motor behaviour.

Dorsal striatum

Widely connected to various neuroreceptor producing centres, the dorsal striatum plays a global role in control. Anatomically, the fibres of the internal capsole (remember: the white matter tract connecting the broader cortex to the thalamus, striatum, and the pons (and from there to the body at large); as well as connecting the putamen to the nucleus caudatus) run in between and around the dorsal striatum. They gather, and underneath the tail form cerebral peduncle, then the crus cerebri and then the basis pontis / pyramidae medollae oblongata.

Nucleus Caudatus

Like the putamen, the nucleus caudatus is a key connector in the cortico-subcortico-cortico, and definitely in the nigrostratial pathway. That means that it's key relayer of dopamine. Thus, is plays role in learning, emotion, sleep and movement:

  1. Motor behaviour: selection and inhibition of movement
  2. Learning: spatial memory
  3. Other: sleep and emotion

Amygdala

Given the anatomical location as almost an extension of the nucleus caudatus, a little bit about the amygdala (Gr. "almond" or "tonsil"). A part of the limbic system, it plays an important role in emotion, and learning and memory. Here, we find hemispheric specialisation:

  1. Right amygdala: negative emotions
  2. Left amygdala: positive emotions

The essence is as follows: the amygdala consists of a number of nuclei:

  1. Basolateral nuclei receive information
  2. Central nucleus sends out efferent pathways
    1. Anterior commisure: olfactory bolb, amygdala, temporal lobe
    2. Stria terminalis: septal nuclei, hypothalamus, thalamus, amygdala
    3. Amygdalofugal: septal nuclei, hypothalamus, thalamus, amygdala, brainstem, basal ganglia, nucleus accumbens

It's essential role is in:

  1. Emotional experience
  2. Learning: conditioning, and long-term potentiation (possibly mediated by emotions)

Putamen

Like a shell, the putamen surrounds the medial globus palllidus. The two are so closely related, they used to be called the Nucleus Lentiformis, as they were shaped like a little bean, but they have been found to be separated by a little bit of white matter, making them into two separate nuclei. The putamen (lat. "nutshell" or "that what falls off when pruning") arises from the forebrain / prosencephalon. Broadly connected, the putamen, and dorsal striatum at large, is part of two major pathways:

  1. Cortico-subcortico-cortico pathway
  2. Nigrostriatial pathway
In the latter, dopamine from the pars compacta of the substantia nigra arrives in the dorsal striatum from whence it plays a role in motor behaviour and even addiction. The putamen is thus hypothesised to play a role in:
  1. Motor behaviour: selection, automaticity, sequence learning
  2. Learning: reinforcement/implicit learning, category learning

Nucleus Accumbens

While the dorsal striatum is intricately connected with the substantia nigra, the ventral tegmental area is more intricately connected with the ventral striatum ... which somehow sounds sensible. The nucleus accumbens then receives afferents from the VTA, and gives back efferents to:
  1. Ventral pallidum -> Thalamus -> Prefrontal cortex
  2. Feedback efferents to the brainstem

Given that it receives projections from the VTA instead of the substantia nigra, there is no role in motor behaviour. Instead there are large roles in learning, emotion and addiction, and the NA may be implicated in depression, substance addiction and the placebo effect.

Olfactory Tubercle

Also innervated by the VTA, this area is much less known than the NA. It is hypothesised to also play a role in learning, reward etc. etc. but may specifically play a role in sensory integration as well as stereotyped behaviour / behavioural flexibility

Raphe nuclei and serotonin pathway

The idea is that the raphe nuclei (Gr. seam) which are distributed in a straight line down the entire mid- and hindbrain are responsible for brain serotonin. The lower nuclei send projections to the body and cerebellum, whicle the upper nuclei send projections to the brain. Given the various projections, the raphe nuclei are implicated in a large number of behaviorus, but most of them are negative.
