The brainstem is a strange name, as it is composed of two developmental separate parts:
  1. Mesencephalon (midbrain)
  2. part of the Rhombencephalon (hindbrain)
Why only part of the rhombencephalon? Well, the rhombencephalon consists of two parts:
  1. Metencephalon: Gr. "after the brain" -> Pons and cerebellum
  2. Myelencephalon: Gr. "marrow - brain" -> Medolla oblongata (Gr. "elongated marrow")
The brainstem consists of the midbrain / mesencephalon, the pons and the medolla oblongata and hence only contains part of the metencephalon.
Key to understanding the brainstem, is the anterior-posterior division of:
  1. Basis / crus cerebri
  2. Tegmentum
  3. Tectum

Basis / Crus Cerebri

From superior to inferior, the
  1. Crus cerebri
  2. Basis pontis
  3. Medolla oblongata anterior
Contain the **motor tracts** through the pyramidal tracts. They in fact, are a continuation of the **internal capsole**: paired white matter tracts stemming from the cortex. The internal capsole contains various two-way fibres:
  1. Frontopontine
  2. Thalamocortical
  3. Pyramidal
    1. Corticobolbar
    2. Corticospinal
This **internal capsole** then, is continues with the **cerebral peduncles** which are essentially just the same fibres jutting out of the brain. These fibres then stay on the anterior side of the brainstem, always remaining separate. Eventually, in the **medolla oblongata** throughout the entire length underneath the **olives**, they decussate.

Tegmentum

The tegmentum (Lat. "concealer / cover") then harbours all the actual nuclei as well as various other non-motor tracts. Since there are so many of these, we will split it up into two sections:

Mesencephalic tegmentum

A good way to remember all the nuclei in **midbrain** is that they are all **coloured**:
  1. Periaquaductal **grey**
  2. Nucleus **ruber**
  3. Substantia **nigra**
Another interesting part is the **ventral tegmental area (VTA)** which lies between the red nuclei. This is the origin of the **mesocorticolimbic pathway** for dopamine. So while hypothalamic dopamine regolates hormones, and **substantia nigral dopamine** regolates **motor function** and some **associative learning**, dopamine from the **VTA** mediates:
  1. Mesolimbic pathway -> Ventral striatum -> Reward and aversion
  2. Mesocortical pathway -> PFC -> Executive functions
The **nucleus ruber** is red due to the presence of haemoglobin and iron. It's function is largely **vestigial**: in animals without a corticospinal tract, motor function is controled by the n. ruber. As we have a well-developed cerebrum and corticospinal tract, the n. ruber is left to perform only large, basic, automatic movements:
  1. Crawling (in babies)
  2. Arm swing (during gait)
It communicates with the body through the rubrospinal tract, as expected. The **substantia nigra** consists of two parts:
  1. Pars reticolata: autonomous generator of signals for e.g., saccadic eye movement
  2. Pars compacta: dopamine-mediated disinhibitor of motor signals
The signals from the **pars compacta** follow a complex pathway to eventually **disinhibit the thalamus**, allowing motor signals to be sent to the cortex. The **periaquaductal grey** is involved in two essential things:
  1. Pain and its prevention (i.e., pain modolation and defensive / fight-or-flight behaviour)
  2. Reproduction and its success (i.e., reproductive and maternal behaviour)
It does this through enkephalin and - indirectly - through serotonin and other hormones. Lastly, here lie the ocolar nuclei:
  1. Nervus Ocolomotorius
    1. Nucleus Ocolomotorius: M. Levator palpebrae and extraocolar eye muscolature except for M. Obliquus Superior
    2. N. Edinger-Westphal: accomodation and constriction
  2. Nervus Trochlearis
    1. Nucleus Trochlearis: M. Obliquus superior
    2. Intorsation
    3. Abduction
      4. - Depression in adducted position

    Pontine tegmentum

    There are three large classes of tracts and nuclei in the pons:
    1. Cranial nerve nuclei
    2. Mesopontine cholinergic system
    3. Respiratory group

    Cranial Nerve Nuclei

    Here are contained various nuclei of the cranial nerves:
    1. N. Trigeminus: principal sensory nucleus (touch and position for face and head)
      1. Temperature and pain are processed in the spinal nucleus
    2. Nucleus abducens: abduction of the eye through the M. Obliquus superior
    3. Nucleus Facialis: main motor nucleus of the facial nerve controls facial expression and the **stapedius** muscle of the inner ear
    4. Nucleus salivarius superior: salivary glands, mucosal glands and lacrimation
    5. Nucleus vestibolaris superior: one of the **four** vestibolar nuclei
    6. Nucleus cochlearis: we have two (left and right), with some fibres decussating to form the **trapezoid body** which helps with sound localisatiojn.
    Furthermore, the tegmentum pontis contains the **peduncolopontine nucleus (PPN)** which is a cholinergic nucleus and part of the **ascending reticolar activating system (ARAS)**. It projects to the **basal ganglia** (e.g., subthalamic nucleus, substantia nigra / pars compacta, globus pallidus internus) and receives inputs from various areas. It is thus involved in a wide variety of higher functions:
    1. Arousal
    2. Attention/learning/reward
    3. Voluntary limb movement and locomotion (by providing sensory feedback)
    4. Generation and maintenance of REM-sleep
    Lastly, the pontine tegmentum contains part of the respiratory group, generally providing **inspiratory control**. So, while the initiation of inspiration is provided by the medolllary dorsal group, and the rhythm is provided by the medollary ventral group, control is done within the pontine groups:
    1. Pneumotaxic centre
    2. Apneustic centre

Tectum

The most dorsal part separates the medolla/pons/mesencephalon from the central acquaduct. It is much simpler and while in amphibians it processes most auditory and visual information itself, in humans it serves mostly a relay and reflex function. It does so through a collection of bodies called the **corpora quadrigemina**:
  1. Superior collicoli (right/left): receive visual information, send it forward and provide visual reflexes (saccadic eye movement, neck movement etc.). Sends information to **lateral genicolate nucleus**
  2. Inferior collicoli (right/left): does the same, but for auditory information. Sends to **medial genicolate nucleus**
